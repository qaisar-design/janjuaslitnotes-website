What is inside

What is inside these English literature study guides?

Every book in the series is built on one plan: the full text made plain, the answers ready to learn, and revision for the last week. Once you know one book, you know them all.

01

Every line in plain modern English

The complete original text, with a plain modern English line under every line and word notes beneath. These are annotated editions with modern English, so you never have to guess what a line means.

02

The answers examiners look for

Every unit has a character or scene card, a background box, a critical note, three short answers, a model context answer and five objective questions. Twelve model essays answer the questions examiners set most often, each with a marked shorter version.

03

Revision for the last week

Practice questions, one-line facts, a quotation bank and a last-night checklist, so the final days go on revising, not searching. Original drawings of every character, in one house style, help you picture who is who.

Questions and answers

Questions students ask

Short answers about the series, the books and the free samples.

What is Janjua’s English Lit Notes?

Janjua’s English Lit Notes is a series of English literature study guides by Qaisar Iqbal Janjua: exam study editions of the works taught worldwide. Each book gives the complete original text with a plain modern English line under every line, word notes, model answers for every section and twelve model essays.

Who are these study guides for?

They are for students of English literature in schools, colleges and universities, wherever English literature is taught. The books name no country, university or exam board; the essays answer the questions examiners set most often on each work.

Which books are in the series so far?

Two books are ready: The General Prologue to The Canterbury Tales by Geoffrey Chaucer, and Hamlet by William Shakespeare, each in an Exam Edition. Othello, Paradise Lost (Books I and IX), Doctor Faustus, The Rape of the Lock, John Donne’s poems and The Importance of Being Earnest are planned next.

When will the books be on Amazon?

Both books are being prepared for Amazon now, as a paperback (6 x 9 in) and a Kindle ebook, and there is no release date yet. Send a short email from a book’s page and you will hear as soon as it is out.

Is there a modern English version of every line?

Yes. Under every line of the original there is a plain modern English line, and the hard words are explained in word notes beneath each passage. Chaucer’s “Than longen folk to goon on pilgrimages” (line 12), for example, has the modern line “Then folk feel the urge to go on pilgrimage”.

Can I read a sample before the books are out?

Yes. Each book has a free PDF sample with the title page, A Note from the Author, the contents and one complete unit: the Wife of Bath from the General Prologue, and the “To be, or not to be” scene from Hamlet. The samples download directly, with no sign-up.

Which edition of the text do the books use?

The General Prologue is printed from Skeat’s edition (Oxford, 1900), and Hamlet from the Cambridge Shakespeare, edited by W. G. Clark and W. A. Wright (1866). Every quotation and line number is checked against that source edition.

Is the series linked to a university or exam board?

No. Janjua’s English Lit Notes is an independent study aid. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to any university, examination board or publisher.
