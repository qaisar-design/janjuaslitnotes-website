The play in one page

What happens in Hamlet?

Hamlet is a revenge tragedy in five acts: the Ghost of Hamlet’s father reveals that Claudius murdered him, and the Prince’s long, troubled revenge ends in a duel that kills Gertrude, Claudius, Laertes and Hamlet. Here is the whole play act by act, as the book sums it up.

  1. Act 1: the Ghost’s command Units 1 to 5

    At midnight on the castle walls of Elsinore, the guards show Horatio a Ghost like the dead King Hamlet. Claudius, the dead king’s brother, has taken the crown and married the widow, Gertrude. The Ghost tells Prince Hamlet that Claudius murdered him, and Hamlet swears revenge and plans to act mad.

  2. Act 2: watchers and players Units 6 and 7

    Polonius decides that love for his daughter Ophelia has driven the Prince mad, and Claudius sends for Rosencrantz and Guildenstern to spy on him. When the Players arrive, Hamlet plans a test: “The play’s the thing / Wherein I’ll catch the conscience of the king” (2.2.580-581).

  3. Act 3: the trap and the turn Units 8 to 11

    Hamlet asks “To be, or not to be” (3.1.56) and turns on Ophelia while the King and Polonius watch. The Players act a murder like his father’s, and Claudius stops the play. Hamlet spares the King at prayer, then kills the hidden Polonius in his mother’s closet.

  4. Act 4: Hamlet sent away Units 12 to 15

    Claudius ships Hamlet to England with letters that order his death. Ophelia goes mad with grief, and Laertes returns to avenge his father. Pirates bring Hamlet back, and Claudius and Laertes plot his death in a fencing match; then Gertrude reports that Ophelia has drowned.

  5. Act 5: the grave and the duel Units 16 and 17

    In the graveyard Hamlet holds Yorick’s skull and fights Laertes in Ophelia’s grave. In the fencing match a poisoned sword and a poisoned cup kill Gertrude, Claudius, Laertes and Hamlet, and Fortinbras arrives to claim Denmark.

Shakespeare wrote Hamlet about 1599 to 1601. It was first printed in 1603, in the short First Quarto; the longer Second Quarto followed in 1604, and the First Folio in 1623.

The units

Hamlet summary, scene by scene: the 17 units

The book gives one unit to each scene or group of scenes: 17 units in all, from the Ghost on the castle walls to the duel. Each line below is the book’s own summary of the scene.

  1. 1

    Unit 1: The Ghost Appears Act 1, Scene 1 · 175 lines

    At midnight on the castle walls, the guards show Horatio a Ghost that looks like the dead king. It will not speak to them, so they decide to tell young Hamlet.

  2. 2

    Unit 2: A Grieving Prince Act 1, Scene 2 · 257 lines

    Claudius, now king and married to his brother’s widow, rules the court with smooth words. Hamlet, still in black, wishes he could die, then learns that a ghost in his father’s shape walks at night.

  3. 3

    Unit 3: Advice and Warnings Act 1, Scene 3 · 136 lines

    Laertes, about to sail for France, warns Ophelia against Hamlet. Polonius gives his son rules for life, then orders his daughter to stop seeing Hamlet.

  4. 4

    Unit 4: The Ghost Beckons Act 1, Scene 4 · 91 lines

    At midnight, while the King feasts inside, Hamlet keeps watch with Horatio and Marcellus. The Ghost appears and beckons, and Hamlet follows it against his friends' warnings.

  5. 5

    Unit 5: The Ghost’s Command Act 1, Scene 5 · 191 lines

    Alone with Hamlet, the Ghost names his murderer and demands revenge. Hamlet vows to remember, swears his friends to silence and plans to seem mad.

  6. 6

    Unit 6: Polonius’s Spy Act 2, Scene 1 · 120 lines

    Polonius sends Reynaldo to spy on Laertes in Paris. Then a frightened Ophelia describes Hamlet’s wild, silent visit, and Polonius decides that love has driven the Prince mad.

  7. 7

    Unit 7: Players at Elsinore Act 2, Scene 2 · 581 lines

    Claudius sets Hamlet’s old friends to spy on him, and Polonius blames love for his madness. Then the Players arrive, and Hamlet plans to test the King with a play.

  8. 8

    Unit 8: To Be, or Not to Be Act 3, Scene 1 · 188 lines

    Claudius and Polonius hide to watch Hamlet meet Ophelia. Hamlet, alone as he believes, weighs life against death, then turns on Ophelia.

  9. 9

    Unit 9: The Mousetrap Act 3, Scene 2 · 382 lines

    Hamlet coaches the Players and asks Horatio to watch the King. The Players act a murder like old Hamlet’s; Claudius rises, the play breaks off, and Hamlet sets off to see his mother.

  10. 10

    Unit 10: Claudius at Prayer Act 3, Scene 3 · 98 lines

    Claudius orders Hamlet to England, then kneels and tries to pray. Hamlet finds him on his knees, but puts his sword away to wait for a worse revenge.

  11. 11

    Unit 11: The Closet Scene Act 3, Scene 4 · 217 lines

    Hamlet kills Polonius through the arras, then forces his mother to compare her two husbands. The Ghost returns, seen only by Hamlet.

  12. 12

    Unit 12: The Body and the Banishment Act 4, Scenes 1 to 3 · 45, 30 and 67 lines

    Hamlet hides the body of Polonius and mocks his school friends. Claudius ships him to England with a secret order for his death.

  13. 13

    Unit 13: Fortinbras’s Army Act 4, Scene 4 · 66 lines

    Fortinbras marches to fight for a worthless patch of Poland. Hamlet, bound for England, measures himself against him.

  14. 14

    Unit 14: Ophelia’s Madness Act 4, Scene 5 · 214 lines

    Ophelia, driven mad by grief, sings of death and lost love before the court. Laertes breaks in to avenge his father, and Claudius talks him round.

  15. 15

    Unit 15: The Plot against Hamlet Act 4, Scenes 6 and 7 · 31 and 196 lines

    Hamlet’s letters announce his return to Denmark. Claudius and Laertes plot his death in a fencing match, and Gertrude brings word that Ophelia has drowned.

  16. 16

    Unit 16: The Graveyard Act 5, Scene 1 · 287 lines

    Two Clowns joke as they dig Ophelia’s grave. Hamlet, back in Denmark, holds Yorick’s skull, then fights Laertes in the open grave.

  17. 17

    Unit 17: The Duel Act 5, Scene 2 · 390 lines

    Hamlet tells Horatio how he sent Rosencrantz and Guildenstern to the death meant for him. He dies in the poisoned duel, but kills Claudius first, and Fortinbras arrives to claim Denmark.

The characters

Hamlet character analysis: who is who in the play?

Hamlet turns on a small circle of characters: a prince, his mother, his uncle the King, and the family of Polonius. The book draws fifteen of them in one house style; here they are with the book’s own notes on each.

Hamlet

Prince of Denmark, son of the dead King Hamlet and of Gertrude. Commanded to avenge his father, he delays, kills Polonius by mistake, and kills Claudius only as he himself dies.

Hamlet, alone

Hamlet in his soliloquies, the speeches he makes alone on stage or unheard by others, where he thinks aloud about grief, revenge and death.

Claudius

King of Denmark. He poisoned his brother, Hamlet’s father, took the crown and married the widow. Hamlet kills him in the last scene.

Gertrude

Queen of Denmark and Hamlet’s mother, who married Claudius soon after her husband’s death. She drinks the poisoned cup meant for her son.

The Ghost

The spirit of Hamlet’s father, the dead King Hamlet. He reveals his murder, commands revenge, and appears for the last time in the closet scene, seen only by Hamlet.

Polonius

The Lord Chamberlain, father of Laertes and Ophelia: a wordy old man and a busy spy. Hamlet kills him through the arras.

Laertes

Polonius’s son, who lives in France. He returns to avenge his father, plots with Claudius, and dies by his own poisoned sword, forgiving Hamlet.

Ophelia

Polonius’s daughter, loved by Hamlet. She obeys her father and rejects Hamlet; after her father’s death she goes mad and drowns.

Horatio

Hamlet’s calm, loyal friend and fellow-student from Wittenberg. He sees the Ghost, watches the King at the play, and lives to tell Hamlet’s story.

Rosencrantz and Guildenstern

Hamlet’s old school friends, sent for by Claudius to spy on him. On the voyage to England Hamlet forges a new royal letter that sends them to their deaths.

Fortinbras

Prince of Norway, whose father was killed by old Hamlet in a duel. At the end Hamlet gives him his “dying voice”, his last vote, for the crown.

Osric

A showy young courtier. He brings the King’s wager to Hamlet and calls the hits in the fencing match.

The Gravedigger

The First Clown in this text: a witty sexton who digs Ophelia’s grave and shows Hamlet the skull of Yorick.

The Player King

One of the Players who come to Elsinore. Hamlet uses their play, The Mousetrap, to catch the conscience of the King.

Marcellus and Bernardo

Officers of the watch, who show Horatio the Ghost on the castle walls in the first scene.

Questions and answers

Hamlet questions students ask

Short, direct answers taken from the Hamlet study guide itself.

Does this book include the full text of Hamlet?

Yes. It prints the complete play from the Cambridge Shakespeare, edited by W. G. Clark and W. A. Wright (1866), in 17 units, one for each scene or group of scenes. Under every line there is a plain modern English line, with notes on hard words after each passage.

Is there a modern English translation of every line of Hamlet?

Yes. Every line of the play has a plain modern English line beneath it. “To be, or not to be: that is the question” (3.1.56), for example, becomes “To live, or not to live: that is the question”.

What are the most important themes in Hamlet?

The book follows six themes through the play: revenge, seeming (appearance against reality), death, madness, corruption and love. Every point on a scene card carries its theme tag, and each theme leads to the essays that need it; revenge, for example, runs through the essays on the foils, the revenge tragedy and the delay.

Which Hamlet essay questions come up most often?

The book’s twelve model essays answer the Hamlet questions examiners set most often: Hamlet as a tragic hero, Claudius, the foils, Ophelia, Gertrude, the soliloquies, dramatic craft, revenge tragedy, madness, the play within the play, the delay, and appearance and reality. Each essay is written in full for long papers, with a marked cut line where a shorter answer can stop.

Is Hamlet’s madness real or feigned?

The book’s answer is that the madness is feigned but the melancholy beneath it is real. Hamlet’s “antic disposition” (1.5.172) is chosen, announced and controlled, and it serves his purpose of watching and wounding his enemies; beneath it lies a real melancholy, and at Ophelia’s grave a real passion. He is mad in craft, but heartsick in fact.

Why does Hamlet delay his revenge?

Not because he lacks courage, the book argues, but because his courage serves a mind that must know, judge and feel before it acts. The need for proof explains the first delay, conscience the second, and melancholy and too much thought the rest.

Who dies at the end of Hamlet?

Four characters die in the last scene, in this order: Gertrude, Claudius, Laertes and Hamlet. Gertrude drinks the poisoned cup meant for Hamlet, Laertes and Hamlet are both wounded by the poisoned sword, and Hamlet kills Claudius with the sword and the cup. Horatio lives to tell the story, and Fortinbras arrives to claim Denmark.

Will the line numbers match my edition of Hamlet?

Not always. The book keeps the line numbers of the Cambridge edition, and other editions number some lines differently, by many lines in the long prose scenes. Examiners name passages by act and scene, so find the scene first, then the words.
