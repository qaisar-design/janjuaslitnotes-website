The play in one page

What happens in Macbeth?

Macbeth is a tragedy in five acts: three Witches promise a Scottish general that he will be king, he murders King Duncan to make it come true, and his rule of blood ends when Macduff kills him. Here is the whole play act by act, as the book sums it up.

  1. Act 1: the prophecies and the plan Units 1 to 4

    Three Witches plan to meet Macbeth. A wounded Sergeant tells Duncan, King of Scotland, how bravely his general Macbeth has fought, and Duncan gives Macbeth the title of the traitor Thane of Cawdor; a thane is a Scottish lord. On a heath the Witches hail Macbeth as Thane of Glamis, Thane of Cawdor and “king hereafter” (1.3.50). They tell his fellow general Banquo, “Thou shalt get kings, though thou be none” (1.3.67). When Ross brings the title, Macbeth begins to think of murder. Duncan names his elder son, Malcolm, as his heir. At Inverness Lady Macbeth reads her husband’s letter and calls on evil spirits to “unsex me here” (1.5.40). Duncan comes to stay. Macbeth decides not to kill him, but his wife mocks his courage, and he gives way: “I am settled” (1.7.79).

  2. Act 2: the murder of Duncan Units 5 and 6

    After midnight Macbeth sees “A dagger of the mind” (2.1.38) and kills the sleeping King. He dares not take the daggers back, so Lady Macbeth does it and smears the grooms with Duncan’s blood. The drunken Porter pretends to keep the gate of hell. Macduff finds the King dead, and Macbeth kills the two grooms. Duncan’s sons flee, Malcolm to England and Donalbain to Ireland, and Macbeth goes to Scone to be crowned.

  3. Act 3: Banquo’s murder and Banquo’s Ghost Units 7 to 9

    Now king, Macbeth fears Banquo and the Witches’ promise to Banquo’s children. His murderers kill Banquo, but Fleance escapes. At a feast Banquo’s Ghost, seen only by Macbeth, sits in his place. Macbeth decides to go on: “I am in blood / Stepp’d in so far” (3.4.136-137) that turning back is as hard as going forward. Hecate plans to ruin him with false hopes. Lennox hears that Macduff has gone to England for help.

  4. Act 4: the apparitions and the murder of Macduff’s family Units 9 to 11

    Three apparitions tell Macbeth to beware Macduff, that “none of woman born / Shall harm Macbeth” (4.1.80-81), and that he is safe until Birnam wood comes to Dunsinane. A show of eight kings shows that Banquo’s heirs will rule. Macbeth has Macduff’s wife and children murdered. In England Malcolm tests Macduff’s loyalty; an English army under Siward is ready. Ross brings the news of the murders, and Macduff vows revenge.

  5. Act 5: the fall of Macbeth Units 12 and 13

    At Dunsinane Lady Macbeth walks in her sleep and tries to wash away blood that only she can see: “Out, damned spot! out, I say!” (5.1.31). Malcolm’s soldiers carry boughs from Birnam wood to hide their numbers. Lady Macbeth dies, and life seems to Macbeth “a tale / Told by an idiot, full of sound and fury, / Signifying nothing” (5.5.26-28). He meets Macduff, who was “from his mother’s womb / Untimely ripp’d” (5.8.15-16). Macduff kills him, and Malcolm is hailed “King of Scotland!” (5.8.59).

Shakespeare wrote Macbeth about 1606. Its only early text is the First Folio of 1623, and it is the shortest of his tragedies: five acts and 28 scenes.

The units

Macbeth scene by scene: the 13 units

The book gives one unit to each scene or group of scenes: 13 units in all, from the Witches’ first meeting to the fall of Macbeth. Each line below is the book’s own summary of the scene.

  1. 1

    Unit 1: The Witches and the Bleeding Sergeant Act 1, Scenes 1 and 2 · 11 and 69 lines

    Three Witches plan to meet Macbeth. A wounded Sergeant praises Macbeth’s courage, and Duncan gives Macbeth the traitor Cawdor’s title.

  2. 2

    Unit 2: The Witches’ Prophecies Act 1, Scene 3 · 156 lines

    The Witches hail Macbeth as Glamis, Cawdor and future king, and promise Banquo royal heirs. Ross brings the Cawdor title; Macbeth thinks of murder.

  3. 3

    Unit 3: Lady Macbeth, the Letter and “Unsex Me Here” Act 1, Scenes 4 and 5 · 58 and 72 lines

    Duncan names Malcolm his heir; Macbeth hides dark wishes. Lady Macbeth reads his letter and prays to evil spirits for cruelty.

  4. 4

    Unit 4: “If It Were Done”: Macbeth Decides Act 1, Scenes 6 and 7 · 31 and 82 lines

    Duncan praises the castle’s sweet air. Macbeth argues himself out of the murder, but his wife shames him and gives him a plan.

  5. 5

    Unit 5: The Dagger and the Murder of Duncan Act 2, Scenes 1 and 2 · 64 and 74 lines

    After midnight Macbeth sees a dagger in the air. He kills Duncan; his wife returns the daggers he dares not take back.

  6. 6

    Unit 6: The Porter and the Discovery of the Murder Act 2, Scenes 3 and 4 · 143 and 41 lines

    A drunken Porter plays hell’s gatekeeper. Macduff finds Duncan murdered; Macbeth kills the grooms and is soon named king. Duncan’s sons flee.

  7. 7

    Unit 7: The Murder of Banquo Act 3, Scenes 1 to 3 · 219 lines

    Now king, Macbeth fears Banquo and his promised heirs. He hires murderers, telling his wife nothing; Banquo dies, but Fleance escapes.

  8. 8

    Unit 8: The Banquet and Banquo’s Ghost Act 3, Scene 4 · 144 lines

    At his feast Macbeth hears that Banquo is dead but Fleance has escaped. Banquo’s Ghost, seen only by him, sits in his place, then returns.

  9. 9

    Unit 9: Hecate and the Apparitions Act 3, Scenes 5 and 6; Act 4, Scene 1 · 241 lines

    Hecate plans Macbeth’s ruin. Lennox mocks the tyrant. Apparitions prophesy, and Macbeth sees Banquo’s royal heirs.

  10. 10

    Unit 10: The Murder of Macduff’s Family Act 4, Scene 2 · 84 lines

    Lady Macduff accuses her absent husband of deserting his family. A Messenger warns her to flee; then Macbeth’s murderers kill her son.

  11. 11

    Unit 11: Malcolm and Macduff in England Act 4, Scene 3 · 240 lines

    Malcolm tests Macduff by pretending to be worse than Macbeth, then says an English army is ready. Ross reports that Macduff’s family has been slaughtered.

  12. 12

    Unit 12: The Sleepwalking Scene Act 5, Scene 1 · 74 lines

    A Doctor and a Gentlewoman watch Lady Macbeth sleepwalk. She rubs at blood only she can see and relives the murders.

  13. 13

    Unit 13: Tomorrow, and Tomorrow: the Fall of Macbeth Act 5, Scenes 2 to 8 · 280 lines

    Deserted, Macbeth faces Malcolm’s army. His queen dies, Birnam wood seems to move, and Macduff kills him.

The characters

Macbeth character analysis: who is who in the play?

Macbeth turns on a small circle of characters: a general and his wife, the King they murder, the Witches who tempt him, and the men who bring him down. The book has fourteen drawings in one house style; here they are with the book’s own notes on each.

Macbeth

A Scottish general, Thane of Glamis and then of Cawdor. Tempted by the Witches and urged on by his wife, he murders King Duncan, rules as a tyrant and is killed by Macduff.

Macbeth, alone

After midnight Macbeth sees a dagger in the air. He kills Duncan; his wife returns the daggers he dares not take back.

Lady Macbeth

Macbeth’s wife. She drives him to the murder, then breaks under guilt, walks in her sleep and dies off stage.

Lady Macbeth, sleepwalking

A Doctor and a Gentlewoman watch Lady Macbeth sleepwalk. She rubs at blood only she can see and relives the murders.

The Witches

Three “weird sisters” (1.3.32) who foretell Macbeth’s future.

Banquo and Fleance

Macbeth’s fellow general and his son. The Witches promise that Banquo’s descendants will be kings, so Macbeth has him murdered, but Fleance escapes. Banquo’s Ghost appears at the feast.

The Ghost of Banquo

At his feast Macbeth hears that Banquo is dead but Fleance has escaped. Banquo’s Ghost, seen only by him, sits in his place, then returns.

Duncan

King of Scotland, gracious and too trusting, murdered in his sleep as Macbeth’s guest.

The Porter

The drunken gatekeeper, who pretends to keep the gate of hell.

The Murderers

Now king, Macbeth fears Banquo and his promised heirs. He hires murderers, telling his wife nothing; Banquo dies, but Fleance escapes.

Hecate

The Witches’ mistress, who scolds them in 3.5 and joins them in 4.1.

Lady Macduff and her son

Macduff’s wife and their clever young son, murdered at their castle in Fife.

Macduff

Thane of Fife. He finds Duncan’s body, flees to England, loses his family to Macbeth’s murderers and kills Macbeth.

Malcolm and Donalbain

Duncan’s sons. Malcolm, the elder, named Prince of Cumberland, flees to England, tests Macduff and leads the army against Macbeth. Donalbain flees to Ireland.

“Fair is foul, and foul is fair. / Hover through the fog and filthy air.”The three Witches1.1.10-11It is the play’s first riddle: good and evil change places, and nothing is what it seems.

Questions and answers

Macbeth questions students ask

Short, direct answers taken from the Macbeth study guide itself.

Does this book include the full text of Macbeth?

Yes. It prints the complete play from the Cambridge Shakespeare, edited by W. G. Clark and W. A. Wright (Volume VII, Macmillan, 1865), in 13 units, one for each scene or group of scenes. Under every line of verse there is a plain modern English line; in the few prose passages, such as the Porter and the sleepwalking scene, the modern English follows the speech as one paragraph.

Is there a modern English translation of every line of Macbeth?

Yes. Every line of verse has a plain modern English line beneath it, and every prose speech is followed by its meaning in modern English. “The multitudinous seas incarnadine” (2.2.62), for example, becomes “Turn the countless seas blood-red”.

What are the most important themes in Macbeth?

The book follows six themes through the play: ambition, the supernatural, guilt, seeming (appearance and reality), kingship and violence. Every point on a scene card carries its theme tag, and each tag leads to the essays that need it; guilt, for example, leads to the essays on Lady Macbeth, on Macbeth and Lady Macbeth, and on guilt and conscience.

Which Macbeth essay questions come up most often?

The book’s twelve model essays answer the Macbeth questions examiners set most often: Macbeth as a tragic hero, Lady Macbeth, Macbeth and Lady Macbeth, the Witches and the supernatural, fate and free will, ambition, guilt and conscience, appearance and reality, kingship and tyranny, the foils, the Porter scene, and imagery. Each essay is written in full for long papers, with a marked cut line where a shorter answer can stop.

Is Lady Macbeth “the fourth witch”?

Only in Act 1, the book argues. There she calls on evil spirits and tempts Macbeth towards the crown, as the Witches do. But she cannot kill Duncan, she finds no joy in the crown, and guilt destroys her: a witch has no conscience to lose, and Lady Macbeth has one. The label fits her part in Act 1, not the whole woman.

Is Macbeth a victim of fate?

No, the book argues. The Witches’ prophecies say what will happen, never how, and every one made to Macbeth comes true; but they never tell him to murder, and Banquo, who hears them too, stays loyal. Macbeth chooses each crime himself: he is not a victim of fate but the maker of his own downfall.

Who kills whom in Macbeth?

Macbeth kills Macdonwald in battle, Duncan, the two grooms and Young Siward. His murderers kill Banquo, while Fleance escapes, and Macduff’s son; Ross reports that Macduff’s wife and children were killed too. Macduff kills Macbeth, and Lady Macbeth’s death is reported, not shown.

Will the line numbers match my edition of Macbeth?

Not always. The book keeps the line numbers of the Cambridge text of 1865, which numbers lines differently from some modern editions, and in Act 5 other editions may divide the scenes differently. Examiners name passages by act and scene, so know each passage by its sense as well as by its number.
